# Project Lego Floor 1.0

> **The floor is the smallest coherent constitutional pattern upon which the present system can keep building without losing the meanings that made the work necessary.**

This document does not replace `VISION.md`, `ONTOLOGY.md`, `INVARIANTS.md`, `EVALUATION.md`, `CONTINUITY.md`, or the executable kernels. It arranges them into one governing pattern, identifies which layer owns which responsibility, and prevents useful ideas from being promoted, flattened, or merged merely because they are nearby.

The floor is versioned because continuity does not require pretending that no future correction will occur. Floor 1.0 is the current bounded settlement. A later floor must preserve explicit lineage to it and name every constitutional change.

## 1. The floor test

Before adding any concept, object, feature, or implementation, ask:

> **Does this piece fit the floor's groove?**

Do not ask whether the piece is impressive, comprehensive, fashionable, easily understood, or capable of proving the entire project.

A piece fits when it:

1. preserves meaningful continuity rather than flattening it;
2. keeps source, projection, authority, and realization in their proper roles;
3. creates explicit lineage for meaningful change;
4. preserves unresolved contradiction and irreducible particulars;
5. remains replaceable where replacement does not alter constitutional meaning;
6. admits deterministic verification for any durable claim it makes;
7. does not silently elevate inference, similarity, reconstruction, or witness into occurrence or authority;
8. belongs clearly to one layer of the floor.

If a piece is valuable but does not yet fit, preserve it as a proposal, tension, experiment, or application module. Do not force it into canon.

## 2. Floor 1.0 in one sentence

> **TranchNode is a model-independent continuity substrate that preserves sufficient relational constraints, exact material residuals, attributable lineage, bounded authority, and unresolved tension so that meaning can lawfully re-emerge across humans, agents, applications, and time without reconstruction impersonating occurrence.**

This sentence integrates the existing vision with the continuity and residual discoveries without changing the established boundaries:

- models interpret and propose;
- the kernel validates durable operations;
- applications remain replaceable;
- retrieval is not authority;
- confidence is not evidence;
- nothing important is flattened into chat history.

## 3. The load-bearing stack

Floor 1.0 has six constitutional layers. Higher layers may depend upon lower layers. No higher layer silently overwrites, redefines, or substitutes for a lower one.

```text
6. Application and participation surfaces
5. Domain kernels and lawful workflows
4. Semantic graph and deterministic evaluation
3. Projection, reconstruction, and continuity machinery
2. Material artifacts and irreducible residuals
1. Constitutional meaning contract
```

### Layer 1 — Constitutional meaning contract

Owned by:

- `VISION.md`
- `INVARIANTS.md`
- `CONTINUITY.md`
- this integration document

This layer defines why the system exists and what no implementation may quietly alter.

Its current canon is:

- continuity is preservation of sufficient relational constraints for lawful re-emergence;
- no silent overwrite;
- no unsupported elevation;
- no source erasure;
- no contradiction destruction;
- no model sovereignty;
- no authority by similarity;
- no scope leakage;
- deterministic replay;
- replaceable intelligence;
- harvest preserves tension;
- reconstruction is not occurrence;
- historically or covenantally irreducible material is not delegated to generative repair without explicit treatment;
- canonicalization is bounded settlement, not final collapse.

Layer 1 changes require a dedicated constitutional pull request. Such a PR must name:

1. the prior rule;
2. the proposed rule;
3. why the prior rule is insufficient;
4. which lower and higher layers are affected;
5. migration and compatibility consequences;
6. new adversarial tests required.

### Layer 2 — Material artifacts and irreducible residuals

Owned by the residual slice in PR #2 and its successors.

This layer preserves the material floor beneath interpretation:

- original artifact bytes are content-addressed directly;
- receipt bodies are addressed separately through canonical serialization;
- no object supplies the hash used to address itself;
- format-specific locators bind residuals to exact parent artifacts;
- exact preservation is verifier-derived, never reconstructor self-report;
- historical status is categorical;
- `violated` is inadmissible under every claim class;
- disclosed omission or transformation may remain lawful for non-historical uses;
- creation is permitted, but it may not impersonate occurrence.

This layer must remain usable without any generative model.

Its next mechanical cuts are:

1. immutable filesystem artifact store;
2. real WAV parsing and exact PCM extraction;
3. transformation receipts with executable or reproducible provenance;
4. artifact custody and verification reports.

Automated residual detection is not part of Floor 1.0. Residual declaration may remain manual. The constitutional requirement is exact treatment after declaration.

### Layer 3 — Projection, reconstruction, and continuity machinery

Owned conceptually by `CONTINUITY.md` and not yet fully implemented.

Its primitive operations are:

1. Observe;
2. Constrain;
3. Reconstruct;
4. Continue.

Its minimal continuity tuple is:

\[
\mathcal{C} = (O, \Gamma, E, R)
\]

where observations and relational constraints interact with an environment or decoder context through a declared lawful reconstruction procedure.

Required machinery:

- `FieldNode` or equivalent root-field representation;
- `ProjectionReceipt`;
- `ReconstructionReceipt`;
- decoder identity and versioning;
- decoder snapshots or reproducible specifications where replay matters;
- closure sets for multi-field ancestry;
- explicit fidelity/status distinctions;
- recursive projection with each step separately receipted.

A projection may become a new observation. It may not become the source it projected.

A reconstruction may be coherent and useful while remaining historically inadmissible.

Criticality, stability/diversity metrics, latent fields, and continuity compression remain research objects until truth closure and decoder versioning are mechanically established.

### Layer 4 — Semantic graph and deterministic evaluation

Owned by:

- `ONTOLOGY.md`
- `EVALUATION.md`
- the accepted-event and claim-evaluation contract on `main`

This is the stable meaning language used to form canonical semantic projections.

Current node kinds:

- `source`
- `observation`
- `claim`
- `inference`
- `proposal`
- `tension`
- `witness`
- `harvest`

These are not asserted to be metaphysical atoms. They are durable semantic forms through which the system records projections and evaluates claims.

Project 0's canonical ontology now contains nine kinds, adding `rejection` as a substantive node. TranchNode v0.1 has no lossless `rejection` representation. This is an explicit cross-project incompatibility, not permission to alias rejection into tension, disposition, receipt, or an existing edge. Any adapter must report the mapping as unavailable or explicitly lossy until a separately versioned TranchNode ontology change is adopted.

Current graph law includes:

- immutable nodes and edges;
- explicit accepted events;
- sequence-authoritative deterministic replay;
- typed edge semantics;
- dispute and supersession without deletion;
- strict scope boundaries;
- deterministic evidence traversal;
- plural current harvests;
- unresolved tensions remain visible;
- source coverage is not confidence or truth scoring.

Layer 4 must eventually receive projection outputs from Layer 3 without confusing:

- field with source;
- projection with occurrence;
- witness with verification;
- harvest with final truth;
- similarity with evidence.

### Layer 5 — Domain kernels and lawful workflows

Owned presently by PR #1's fulfillment minimum lawful slice and future domain modules.

A domain kernel applies the lower constitutional layers to one bounded human purpose. It may introduce domain-specific events, validators, projections, and fixtures. It may not redefine substrate meanings.

The fulfillment kernel currently establishes:

- disclosure policy instead of invented consent abstractions;

- unknown provenance may remain unknown but is never invented;
- canonical events and derived projections never exchange roles;
- the child may remain the telos without becoming telemetry;
- fulfillment records a reported material crossing, not proof of success or moral completion.

The fulfillment kernel is a Floor 1.0 Lego module, not the universal ontology.

Other lawful modules may later include:

- deliberation;
- creative lineage;
- artifact transformation;
- collaborative participation;
- need/offer coordination;
- continuity audit;
- local-first replication.

Each module must declare its constitutional dependencies and its forbidden collapses.

## 6. Anti-drift rules

### Rule A — No layer substitution

A Layer 6 interface cannot define Layer 1 law. A Layer 5 domain object cannot silently become a Layer 4 universal node kind. A Layer 4 harvest cannot overwrite Layer 2 source material.

### Rule B — No semantic alias without mapping

A project-specific term may be used only when its adapter names the canonical type or explicitly declares that no canonical equivalent exists.

Examples:

- Seed may map to proposal, source, or participation object depending on context; it is not one universal substrate kind.
- Trace may map to observation, witness, edge, or receipt; the mapping must be explicit.
- Harvest maps to the canonical harvest meaning and must preserve included tension.
- Donkey privacy is an application boundary unless encoded as a substrate disclosure or scope rule.

### Rule C — No doctrine hidden in code

Any implementation that changes the meaning of source, witness, authority, occurrence, reconstruction, continuity, or admissibility requires a corresponding constitutional document change.

### Rule D — No code hidden in doctrine

A principle is not considered mechanically established until an adversarial test demonstrates its enforcement.

### Rule E — Preserve scars

Irregularities, contradictions, unfinishedness, rough drafts, and particular details are not automatically noise. They must be assessed for residual, evidentiary, historical, identity, or covenantal significance before normalization.

The record is the resistance. The particular is not disposable merely because a smoother summary is available.

### Rule F — Promote by evidence, not enthusiasm

A research brick becomes structural or constitutional only when:

1. its claim is stated narrowly;
2. its dependencies are known;
3. its failure modes are documented;
4. an executable or inspectable test exists;
5. migration consequences are understood;
6. it does not violate existing invariants.

### Rule G — Preserve role distinctions across projects

Current role map:

- TranchNode: continuity and meaning substrate;
- PCL: prospective reasoning grammar;
- Jubilee: authority/memory kernel family;
- NanaSpork: field instrument;
- BananaGram: portable participation seed;
- Full Measure: gamified life layer.

These may converge operationally. They must not be collapsed conceptually until the exact boundary change is named and reviewed.

### Rule H — Deployment is not ontology

Local-first, hosted, Supabase-backed, SQLite-backed, replicated, or centralized operation are deployment choices unless they alter authority, privacy, ordering, or identity semantics.

The current tension between local-first language and later authoritative-kernel work remains explicit. Floor 1.0 does not settle it by omission.

- a single canonical addressing discipline is used across generic substrate receipts;
